UK with and without AI — ramped automation

OG-UK 1-sector, 2026–2030 · γ ramps 0.35→0.389, Z ramps →1.031, ε=1.0 · indexed to baseline 2026 = 100

First run with time-varying γ. Automation now builds to 2030 instead of jumping once, which OG-Core could not do before — gamma was indexed [m] with no time dimension in ~14 sites of firm.py. Three things follow: the paths share a common origin, the labour share becomes a path (0.650 → 0.611) rather than a step, and GDP growth reaches 4.74% against 2.55% under the step — much closer to Anthropic’s 5.4%.

The ramp fixes the growth discrepancy. Under the step, the whole level effect landed in 2026 and there was no growth left to deliver — 2.55% against Anthropic’s 5.4%. With automation building, growth reaches 4.74%. The 2026 gap falls from +12.2% to +4.2%, and the debt effect almost vanishes (+11.85% → +0.72%) because the fiscal position is no longer shocked all at once.
Caveats. (1) 2026 is +4.2%, not zero — households have perfect foresight and respond to the announced path from period one. A true common origin needs an unanticipated shock, which TPI cannot represent. (2) Investment is unreliable — residual in the resource constraint, and the 2030 reversal (+47% → +3%) is numerical, not economic. RC 6.7e-03 / 7.8e-03. (3) g_y_annual = 1.1% is mis-sourced; should be 1.0% (OBR productivity growth). (4) No unemployment, no search, no occupational split — this cannot speak to displacement. (5) The firm.py patch is local to this machine and not yet upstream; legacy behaviour is bit-identical (regression-tested), but it needs a PR to OG-Core.
