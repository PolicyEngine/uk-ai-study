\section{Policy responses}
\label{sec:policy}

If the shock modelled above is structural rather than cyclical---a permanent reallocation of labour demand rather than a downturn that unwinds---then the existing tax-benefit system already cushions part of the loss (Section~\ref{sec:results}: about \genDecompAbsorbedWorstPct~per cent of the market-income loss in the hardest-hit decile, seed 0), and the remaining question is what further reductions in hardship would cost. This section evaluates three stylised policy responses inside the shocked world. Each reform is simulated on top of the central scenario (7 per cent displacement, seed 0, 2026), so the estimand throughout is the difference between the shocked-plus-reform and shocked simulations: what the reform adds, given that the shock has happened. I evaluate them only as responses to the shock, not as standing policy; each is modelled for 2026 alone.

\subsection{Three stylised reforms}
\label{sec:policy-reforms}

The first reform (R1) is a wage-insurance scheme in the spirit of trade-adjustment proposals: displaced workers receive 50 per cent of their lost earnings, capped at \pounds 15{,}000 per year. I implement it as a post-simulation transfer that is non-taxable and disregarded by means tests, so its gross cost equals its net cost; this brackets the generous end of plausible implementations. The second (R2) is a demand-side circuit breaker within the existing architecture: a 20 per cent increase in the Universal Credit standard allowance, implemented as a parameter reform inside PolicyEngine so that all interactions with tapers, caps and passported entitlements are captured. The third (R3) suspends the benefit cap and cuts the UC earnings taper from 55 to 45 per cent, targeting the households for whom the existing system's own restraints bind hardest. R2 and R3 costs are net of clawback through the rest of the system.

The table reports simulated fiscal cost per poverty point averted; it is not a complete welfare or policy appraisal. The UC uplift averts a percentage point of after-housing-costs poverty for \pounds \genRtwoCostPerPpAhcBn~billion, and the cap-and-taper package for \pounds \genRthreeCostPerPpAhcBn~billion, against \pounds \genRoneCostPerPpAhcBn~billion for wage insurance. This pattern reflects the specifications: wage insurance is earnings-proportional, and because the exposure-proportional shock displaces workers who are disproportionately in the upper half of the distribution, roughly \genRoneTopThreeDecileSharePct~per cent of R1 reform pounds accrue to households in baseline deciles 8--10. None of this settles the choice of instrument. Wage insurance addresses consumption smoothing and earnings-loss compensation for displaced workers, whereas the UC reforms target low household income. Table~\ref{tab:policy} compares the designs on poverty averted per pound only. The comparison does not rank the instruments on insurance, consumption smoothing, administration, financing or wider welfare objectives.

\subsection{The tax-composition channel}
\label{sec:policy-tax}

The reforms above add expenditure after the shock has weakened the simulated fiscal position, and the size of that damage depends on a question the microsimulation cannot answer by itself: where does the displaced wage bill go? The March 2026 Economic and Fiscal Outlook does not model AI displacement. It does, however, note more generally that a shift in income growth from labour towards profits depresses receipts because labour income carries a higher effective tax rate \citep{obr2026}. I use that tax-composition mechanism only as motivation and price it with an author-designed accounting sensitivity rather than presenting it as an OBR AI scenario. Let $\phi$ denote the author-chosen share of the displaced wage bill that reappears as corporate profits taxed at the 25 per cent main rate. The exercise isolates the labour-tax gap: for each displacement rate from 1 to 10 per cent it runs its own displacement-only simulation (seed 0) with no wage uplift and the capital shock switched off, so the capital channel's extra tax on interest and dividends does not enter. Its $\phi=0$ shortfall is therefore neither the grid's zero-wage cell, which carries the capital shock, nor the \pounds \genCentralCostBn~billion full-balance central cost (seed 0). At 7 per cent displacement the displaced wage bill is \pounds \genCompWageBillBn~billion and the effective income-tax-plus-NICs rate on displaced workers' earnings is \genCompEffRatePct~per cent, against the 25 per cent corporation-tax main rate. The labour tax forgone is \pounds \genCompLabourTaxBn~billion. On the narrow cash basis (income tax and National Insurance less benefits) the net revenue change is \pounds$\genCompPhiZeroSignedBn$~billion at $\phi=0$, \pounds$\genCompPhiHalfSignedBn$~billion at $\phi=0.5$ and \pounds$\genCompPhiOneSignedBn$~billion at $\phi=1$; at $\phi=1$ the corporation tax differs from full labour taxation by \pounds$\genCompPhiOneGapSignedBn$~billion, and the remainder is shock-induced benefit spending together with smaller non-labour-tax revenue changes. On the government balance the $\phi=0$ change is \pounds$\genCompPhiZeroGovSignedBn$~billion. % results/tax_composition/composition_grid.csv (7 per cent rows)

Revenue neutrality is not necessarily distributional neutrality. At $\phi=0.5$ I simulate an author-chosen payout ratio of 0.5: \pounds \genRecyclingDividendsBn~billion of post-corporation-tax profits are distributed pro rata to existing dividend holders in the microdata, with dividend tax captured in the simulation. Both this run and its comparator apply the central 7 per cent displacement and 2.6 per cent wage uplift with the capital shock switched off. Relative to the shocked world without recycling, the Gini changes by $\genRecyclingGiniChangeSignedPp$ percentage points and BHC and AHC poverty by $\genRecyclingPovBhcPp$ and $\genRecyclingPovAhcPp$ points. % results/tax_composition/recycling_case.json
The exercise illustrates how, under this allocation rule, tax recovery and the distribution of household income move separately.

Two caveats bound this exercise. It is static accounting layered on top of the microsimulation: the incidence of corporation tax is not modelled, the corporation-tax base is assumed equal to accounting profit (no allowances, losses or profit shifting), and the effective labour rate is a pro-rata attribution of income tax and NICs to employment income that ignores the schedule ordering of savings and dividend bands. And $\phi$ itself is the unconstrained parameter the whole channel turns on; I present a grid rather than a point estimate because nothing in my framework pins it down. Relatedly, the $\phi=1$ result is closer to an accounting identity than an empirical finding; the object of interest is the distributional consequence at every $\phi$, not the revenue arithmetic at the endpoint.
