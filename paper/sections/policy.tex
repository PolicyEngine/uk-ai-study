\section{Policy responses}
\label{sec:policy}

The existing system already absorbs part of the shock (Section~\ref{sec:results-wagemargin}). This section prices three stylised additions inside the shocked world. Each is simulated on top of the central scenario for 2026 only, and the estimand is the shocked-plus-reform world minus the shocked world, with poverty lines held at their shocked values.

R1 is a wage-insurance scheme in the spirit of trade-adjustment proposals: displaced workers receive 50 per cent of lost earnings, capped at \pounds 15{,}000 a year. I implement it as a post-simulation transfer that is untaxed and disregarded by means tests, so its gross cost equals its net cost and sits at the top of the range of possible designs. R2 raises the UC standard allowance by 20 per cent, and R3 suspends the benefit cap and cuts the UC taper from 55 to 45 per cent; both are PolicyEngine parameter reforms, so their costs are net of withdrawal elsewhere in the system. The fiscal and distributional aims of these designs differ, as do their administrative requirements; Table~\ref{tab:policy} compares them on cost and on poverty only.
