\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results are scenario analysis. I do not forecast the pace or extent of AI adoption; I trace what the UK tax-benefit system would do if labour-market shocks of a given size, incidence and margin occurred under current rules. The observational record does not yet discriminate between the incidence views: the earliest evidence ranges from minimal effects on earnings and hours \citep{humlum2025} to relative employment declines for young workers in exposed occupations \citep{brynjolfsson2025}, none at the scale of the central shock.

Three findings do not depend on the central parameters. First, who loses the job changes the cost of a fixed head-count shock by a factor of five across the stylised rules, because the Exchequer loses each displaced worker's income tax and NI and the UK tax base leans on the earnings of the occupations most exposed. Second, the margin changes how the shock is shared: the Exchequer absorbs more of each pound lost through wage cuts than through job loss, while job loss concentrates the household loss and raises absolute poverty about ten times as much. Third, measured inequality rises under every displacement rule and grid cell examined, with or without the capital channel. The level and sign of the central cost, by contrast, turn on three assumptions with break-even values close to their central settings (Table~\ref{tab:breakeven}) and on how much of the displaced wage bill returns to UK households as taxable profit (Table~\ref{tab:closure}).

The comparison with the Irish simulation of \citet{doorley2026}, which uses the same central conventions on SILC data and the SWITCH model, shows the same qualitative pattern (Table~\ref{tab:ireland}). The fiscal magnitudes are not comparable, because \citet{doorley2026} report Exchequer effects as shares of a net revenue base that includes welfare spending.

\subsection{Limitations and further work}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Exposure.} Exposure varies across nine SOC2020 major groups through a chained crosswalk, a reconstructed complementarity index and ASHE weights covering 340 of 412 unit groups; the occupation of SPI-drawn top-income records is imputed. Quota rates run from zero (elementary occupations) to about 10 per cent (Table~\ref{tab:jobloss}), so incidence is a between-group gradient. A UK-native index \citep{henseke2025} with detailed occupations imputed from the Labour Force Survey would remove both constraints.

\paragraph{Dynamics and behaviour.} Displacement is a one-year stock with full-year zero earnings, no re-employment, no change in partners' hours, no redundancy pay and no running down of savings. UK evidence on earnings losses after displacement \citep{upward2019} would calibrate spell length and re-employment wages. Contributory Jobseeker's Allowance is not paid to newly displaced workers, because the model carries only reported awards; newly displaced households keep their baseline UC take-up flags; and the pay bill saved when a public-sector employee is displaced is outside the government balance. Reduced hiring of young workers, the margin on which the strongest early evidence lies \citep{brynjolfsson2025, kleinteeselink2025, hosseini2026}, is outside a stock-based framework.

\paragraph{Capital and the self-employed.} The capital channel covers interest and dividends only, and its size depends on the dividend base: Microcosm's 2026 taxpayer dividends exceed the SPI projection by \genDivRatioAbsPct~per cent. The self-employed are outside the labour-market shocks.

\paragraph{Next runs.} Two sets of runs are specified, with code and tests, for the next revision. The first sizes displacement to the OBR's unemployment rise with flat survivor earnings and no capital windfall, records the decile flows behind the stabilisation coefficients, and compares the result with the OBR's fiscal effects line by line. The second replaces the capital uplift with $+10$ and $+20$ per cent uplifts, a dividend-only uplift and the closed account of Section~\ref{sec:results-closure} simulated at $\phi=0.5$ and $\phi=1$.
