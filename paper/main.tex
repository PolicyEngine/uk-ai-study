\begin{abstract}
\noindent How much of an AI labour-market shock does the UK tax-benefit system absorb, and who bears the rest? I link occupation-level AI exposure to workers in Microcosm, a calibrated microdataset built from the 2024--25 Family Resources Survey, and use PolicyEngine UK to run displacement, survivor-wage and capital-income shocks through the tax-benefit system at household level. Two comparisons hold the size of the shock fixed. First, reallocating the same expected number of displaced workers (7 per cent of employees) across four stylised incidence rules changes the annual Exchequer cost from \pounds\genIncUniformCostMeanBn~billion to \pounds\genIncCompressionCostMeanBn~billion; a top-loaded stress test reaches \pounds\genIncKleinCostMeanBn~billion. Second, delivering the same gross earnings loss through wage cuts rather than job losses raises the cost from \pounds\genCentralCostBn~billion to \pounds\genWageMarginCentralCostBn~billion and lowers the rise in absolute poverty from \genCentralPovBhcPp~to \genWageMarginCentralPovBhcAbsPp~percentage points on a paired draw: the Exchequer absorbs \genAbsJobGovPct~pence per pound of earnings lost through job loss and \genAbsWageGovPct~pence through wage cuts. Averaged over 50 draws, the exposure-proportional central case costs \pounds\genCentralMcCostMeanBn~billion a year, the net of a \pounds\genChannelMcDispCostAbsBn~billion displacement cost and receipts from assumed survivor-wage and capital-income gains; it turns into a net gain at a survivor-wage effect of \genBeWagePct~per cent or out-of-work spells of about \genBeDurMonths~months. Its \genCentralMcPovBhcMeanPp-point rise in absolute poverty is an upper bound (\genCentralMcRelPovBhcMeanPp~points on the relative line). Inequality rises under every incidence rule and shock size examined. I compare the results with the OBR's technological-displacement scenario.
\end{abstract}
